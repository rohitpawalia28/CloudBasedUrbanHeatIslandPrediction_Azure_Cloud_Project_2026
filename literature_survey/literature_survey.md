# Literature Survey - 15 Papers

**Student:** Rohit Kumar (23BIT0030)  
**Review approach:** Each paper is reviewed for its method, data, contribution, limitation, and the project-specific opportunity it leaves open. Papers 1-13 are recent (2024-2026 where indicated); Papers 14-15 are foundational references used to support the cloud-geospatial design.

| No. | Paper and method | Dataset / context | Advantages | Limitations | Project-specific research opportunity |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Gaur & Deb (2026), review of ML methods for UHI assessment. | Published UHI and ML studies. | Compares modern learning approaches and evaluation issues. | A review does not provide an end-to-end deployable pipeline. | Implement a reproducible cloud workflow around a selected and explainable prediction model. |
| 2 | *Prediction of urban heat island intensity based on multiple remote sensing indices and land-use simulation* (2025). | Remote-sensing indices and land-use simulation. | Combines multiple environmental indicators. | Future inputs depend on the quality of land-use simulation. | Preserve feature provenance and evaluate against held-out locations and time periods. |
| 3 | *Modelling urban heat island effects: A global analysis of 216 cities* (2025). | Multi-city urban observations. | Broad comparison across many urban contexts. | Global modelling may lose city-level local detail. | Start with a selected city while retaining a design that can scale to more cities. |
| 4 | *Mapping and predicting urban heat island intensity hotspots through remote sensing and machine learning* (2026). | Satellite and urban layers. | Directly targets hotspot prediction. | Prediction quality can vary with cloud cover and local morphology. | Add preprocessing checks, uncertainty reporting, and validation before publishing maps. |
| 5 | *The urban heat island effect: A review on predictive approaches and mitigation strategies* (2025). | UHI prediction and mitigation literature. | Connects predictive work to practical mitigation. | Recommendations are often not tied to an operational cloud system. | Deliver map outputs in a dashboard-ready form for planning interpretation. |
| 6 | *Multi-scenario prediction of future urban heat islands: An improved modelling framework* (2026). | Urban-growth and climate scenarios. | Supports future-oriented planning. | Scenario assumptions can be uncertain and computationally intensive. | First establish a reliable current-condition model, then add scenarios as an extension. |
| 7 | *A global urban heat island intensity dataset: Generation, comparison, and analysis* (2024). | Global UHI intensity dataset. | Enables broad-scale benchmarking and comparison. | Coarser global data may not capture local street-scale conditions. | Use it as a reference layer and supplement it with city-specific satellite features. |
| 8 | *Machine and deep learning-based forecast of Tehran's accelerating nighttime surface heat island crisis* (2026). | Tehran surface-UHI time series. | Examines time-dependent heat behaviour and advanced models. | Deep models may need large, stable training data and higher computation. | Compare a transparent baseline with complex models and select based on measured value. |
| 9 | *Urban heat islands from multiple perspectives: Trends across disciplines and interrelationships* (2024). | Interdisciplinary UHI literature. | Shows the environmental, social, and planning context of UHI. | Broad synthesis does not define a specific cloud deployment. | Keep the system focused on geospatial prediction while making outputs useful for planning. |
| 10 | *Climatic comparison of surface urban heat island using satellite remote sensing in Tehran and suburbs* (2024). | Satellite-derived surface UHI in Tehran. | Highlights urban-rural contrast and satellite analysis. | A static comparison is not equivalent to an automated predictive platform. | Automate data preparation and prediction through managed cloud services. |
| 11 | *Climatology of Tehran surface heat island: A satellite-based spatial analysis* (2025). | Satellite spatial analysis of Tehran. | Provides spatial heat-pattern insight. | Site-specific climatology may not transfer directly to another city. | Make study-area configuration explicit and reusable in the architecture. |
| 12 | *A review of urban heat island mapping approaches with emphasis on machine learning and deep learning* (2025). | UHI mapping literature. | Clarifies modelling choices and data requirements. | Reviews do not solve data-governance or deployment challenges. | Pair model selection with cloud storage, monitoring, and secure API design. |
| 13 | *Recent advances on machine learning techniques for urban heat island applications: A review and new horizons* (2025). | ML techniques for UHI applications. | Identifies emerging methods and future needs. | Accuracy claims vary because studies use different data and validation strategies. | Use consistent MAE, RMSE, R-squared, and documented train-test splits. |
| 14 | Gorelick et al. (2017), Google Earth Engine for planetary-scale geospatial analysis. | Large Earth-observation archives. | Demonstrates scalable cloud geospatial analysis. | Does not provide a UHI-specific AWS deployment. | Use its design lessons for scalable acquisition while retaining the planned AWS processing architecture. |
| 15 | Stewart & Oke (2012), Local Climate Zones for urban temperature studies. | Urban and rural morphology classes. | Provides a standard framework for describing thermal urban environments. | Requires suitable local spatial data and classification effort. | Use morphology and land-cover features to improve interpretation of model outputs. |

## Review conclusion

The surveyed work supports the use of remote sensing, environmental indices, and machine learning for UHI analysis. The implementation opportunity is not merely a new model: it is a carefully documented cloud workflow that manages heterogeneous data, validates predictions, protects access, records model data lineage, and delivers outputs in a useful spatial form.
